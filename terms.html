1. What Roster does

Roster does two things with the Google Ads accounts and Meta ad accounts you connect: it builds client reports from their data, and, when you ask it to, it sets up new campaigns in them. You connect a Google account, a Meta login or both, once for your agency, and choose which of the advertising accounts they reach belong to each client. For a report, you choose which of those accounts and campaigns it covers, and Roster renders it against live data as a dashboard you can share.

Roster reads your advertising accounts to build reports. If you ask it to, it can also set up a Google Ads campaign or conversion action, or a Meta campaign, in an account you have connected. It shows you exactly what it will create, creates it only after you approve that, and creates campaigns paused. It does not edit, pause, remove or switch on anything in your accounts; the one exception is a Meta campaign Meta refuses partway through being created, which Roster removes so that nothing half-made is left.

What you set up through Roster is yours. You are responsible for switching a campaign on, for what it spends, for its ads and targeting complying with Google’s and Meta’s advertising policies and the law, and for any statement made to Google or Meta on your behalf — such as whether a campaign contains EU political advertising, whether it falls in one of Meta’s special ad categories (housing, employment, financial products, politics, gambling), or who an ad shown in the EU is for and who pays for it. Roster always asks you these and never answers them for you. Roster is not responsible for advertising spend, or for Google’s or Meta’s decisions about your ads or accounts.

7. Accuracy of figures

Roster reproduces figures reported by Google Ads and Meta and computes derived rates from them. It calculates ratios from summed totals rather than averaging per-row values, which avoids a common and material class of reporting error.

Even so, the underlying figures are Google's and Meta's, they can be restated after the fact, and reports may be affected by API limits, revoked authorisation or outages. You are responsible for checking any report before you send it to a client, and for the commentary and advice you build on it.
