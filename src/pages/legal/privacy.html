Privacy Policy

Roster is a reporting and campaign setup tool for advertising agencies, for Google Ads and Meta Ads. It reads Google Ads and Meta ads data on your behalf to build reports (and, if you connect them, Search Console and Google Analytics data), and sets up new campaigns when you ask it to. It changes a Google Ads account or a Meta ad account only when you ask it to set up a campaign (or, in Google Ads, a conversion action), and only after you have approved that exact change. This policy explains what it stores, why, and how to get rid of it.

2. What we collect

Account details
Your email address and a password. Passwords are never stored in readable form — we keep only a salted scrypt hash, and cannot recover your password.
Session cookies
One cookie that identifies your signed-in session, and a short-lived cookie used while you connect Google or Meta, to prevent request forgery. Both are httpOnly, so page scripts cannot read them. Neither is used for measurement. See section 8.
Analytics cookies
Google Analytics sets a cookie that gives your browser a random identifier, so that the pages you open in one visit are recognisable as one visit rather than as several unrelated strangers. It holds a random number and nothing else — not your name, not your email, and nothing typed into Roster. Section 8 says what it is for, and how to refuse it.
Client details
The client names you create, and which Google Ads accounts and Meta ad accounts you attach to each. When you ask for a report or a campaign on an ad account that is in none of your clients, Roster puts it in the client you choose, or makes a new client for it, named after the account, which you can keep, rename or delete. These names are typed by you or taken from your ad accounts, and may identify your clients.
Reports you ask for
A report exists from the moment you press Copy for Claude in Roster: the ad account and the campaigns it covers, the note you write for Claude if you write one, and when it was asked for, picked up by Claude, finished or cancelled. Roster shows where it stands on the report’s page and on Home. The note is part of the message you paste into Claude, so Claude reads it (section 6). It shows on the report’s page, for you, while Claude builds it, and never on the links you send your clients.
Files and client profiles
Files you attach to a report (on its job or its page) or to a client (in Client details) — PDF, PowerPoint, Word, Excel, CSV, text or images, up to 25 MB each — and what Roster reads from them when you add them: their text, and pages and pictures as images. A client’s profile, also in Client details: what the business does and sells, its customers and markets, what counts as success and its targets, who reads its reports, what to avoid, its website and anything else you add. You fill it in there, or Claude asks and, on your yes, saves your answers. Claude reads both when it builds that client’s reports, which passes them to Anthropic (section 6). Neither is shown on the links you send your clients. A file is deleted when you remove it, with its report or client, or when you cancel the request it is on.
Branding
If you set them: your agency’s name, logo, browser-tab icon, accent colour, website and contact line, and for each client a logo, a colour and which logos its reports show. Logos are stored in our database. A client’s name is the title on its reports. All of it appears on the share links you send, so treat it as public once a link is out.
Google Ads data
The accounts each Google login you connect can reach — their names and IDs, the manager accounts they sit under, currency, time zone and whether each is a test account — so you can choose which belong to each client. For the accounts you choose: campaign names and IDs, and performance figures such as spend, impressions, clicks and conversions. Rendered reports are stored as dated snapshots so a report keeps its history and a client link does not go blank mid-refresh. For a campaign Roster set up, Roster reads its status and its last 30 days’ spend, clicks and conversions only when you press Check status on its page, or ask Claude how it is doing, and keeps the latest reading, with its time, on that page.
Google account identity
The email address of each Google account used to connect, shown so you can tell your logins apart and see which one reads each client’s accounts, and Google’s ID for that account, so that connecting the same one again replaces its token rather than adding a second.
Search Console data
If you connect Search Console: the sites each Google login you connect can view, so you can choose which belong to each client. For the sites you choose: the organic search figures Google reports for them — clicks, impressions, click-through rate and average position — by date, search query, page, country, device and search appearance. Queries are what people searched, as totals Google reports for the site; Google leaves out rare ones, and none of them identifies a person. Reports keep them as dated snapshots, as they do Google Ads figures.
Google Analytics data
If you connect Google Analytics: the GA4 accounts and properties each Google login you connect can read — their names and IDs, time zone and currency — so you can choose which belong to each client. For the properties you choose: totals such as sessions, users, engagement, page views, events, key events and, where the property records them, revenue and transactions, by date, channel, source and medium, campaign, landing page, page, country, city, device, new or returning visitor, and event name. Roster reads no user-level data. Reports keep these totals as dated snapshots.
Campaigns you set up
When you start a campaign in Roster with Copy for Claude, Roster keeps the request: the client, the ad account, the note you wrote for Claude, when you asked, when Claude first read it, and whether you cancelled it. Each draft that Roster and Google or Meta check is kept on the campaign’s page — its settings, targeting (with the names of its places and languages, and on Meta its Page and pixel), budget and bidding, every ad’s text, the web addresses of its images and videos, Roster’s suggestions (“Things to look at”) and when it was checked; a newer draft replaces it. When you say yes in Claude, Roster keeps the draft as it was at that moment, with the time (“What you approved”), and a record of the change it made in your account — when, and what Google or Meta answered. Images are fetched from the addresses you give and passed to Google or Meta; Roster keeps only a fingerprint of each, not the image. Meta fetches videos from their addresses itself; Roster never holds them. See sections 3 and 4.
Conversion actions you set up
If you ask Roster to set up a Google Ads conversion action: what you approved, the record of the change, and the tag code Google returns for it, kept so that you can copy it again from the campaign’s page.
Comments on campaigns
What anyone signed in to your Roster login writes on a campaign’s page, with a name if they give one, and the comments Claude adds there, which are marked Claude. Claude reads them whenever it works on that campaign, so they reach Anthropic like the rest of the conversation (section 6). Please don’t put personal data about your clients’ customers in them. The name you give is remembered in that browser’s local storage and sent to Roster only with a comment; clearing the browser’s data removes it.
Meta ads data
The ad accounts each Facebook login you connect can reach — their names and IDs, currency, time zone, status, and the business portfolio that owns them or that a client shared them with — so you can choose which belong to each client. For the accounts you choose: campaign names and IDs, and performance figures such as spend, impressions, reach, clicks, video views, engagement and the one conversion action you pick for a report, with breakdowns by age, gender, country, region, platform, placement, device and hour of day. These are totals Meta reports for an ad account; none of them identifies a person who saw an ad. Reports keep them as dated snapshots, as they do Google Ads figures. For a campaign Roster set up, Roster reads its status and its last 30 days’ spend, clicks and conversions only when you press Check status on its page, or ask Claude how it is doing, and keeps the latest reading, with its time, on that page.
Facebook Pages, Instagram accounts and pixels
For the Meta ad accounts you choose: the names and IDs of the Facebook Pages each can advertise as and of the Instagram accounts its ads can run as, the account’s pixels (their names, IDs and when each last received an event), whether it has a payment method (not the method itself) and Meta’s minimum budgets for it — so Roster can tell you what a campaign there needs, and run its ads as the Page you choose. Roster does not read the Pages’ or Instagram accounts’ posts, messages, followers or insights.
Account checks for campaigns
When you pick an ad account in a campaign job, or Claude checks one for you, Roster checks what it can set up there: for a Google Ads account, whether your login can make changes in it, its billing and advertiser verification status, its conversion actions and their counts over the last 30 days, linked Merchant Center and Business Profile accounts, and its YouTube videos; for a Meta ad account, whether your login can make changes in it, its payment method, Pages, Instagram accounts and pixels. The result is kept with that client, and replaced by the next check; for an account in no client, it isn’t kept. To learn whether your login can make changes, Roster sends a request that Google or Meta checks and then discards, changing nothing.
Campaign tracking
When you track campaigns, Roster keeps which campaigns you picked, in which ad account and for which client, and the budget, target and range you set. Once a day, and when you press Check now, it reads those campaigns: their status, whether their ads were approved, the account’s billing state and spending limits, spend and results, and the account’s change history for them, including the name or email address Google or Meta records against each change, so that changes made by your team aren’t reported to you as news. Roster keeps the latest reading, what it found and when each finding was resolved; resolved findings are shown for 30 days. Stopping tracking ends the daily reads; deleting the client deletes all of it.
Your Claude connection
For each Claude, or other assistant, you connect to Roster: a token, stored only as a hash, and what you approved when you connected it — reports, or reports and campaign setup — so it is offered only what you approved. It ends when you sign out of the connection in that application, or after 30 days without use.

The name and Meta user ID of each Facebook account used to connect, shown so you can tell your logins apart and see which one reads each client’s ad accounts.
Meta invite requests
If you ask us to invite you to connect Meta Ads: the Facebook profile link or name you give, when you asked, and when we marked the invite sent. 3. Google user data

You connect Google Ads once, for your agency, in Settings, and the connection serves every client whose accounts it reaches. Google asks you to authorise Roster and then issues us a refresh token for that Google account; you can connect more than one, and each has its own. We store each token encrypted at rest using AES-256-GCM and use it for two purposes: reading the Google Ads data needed to render your reports, and making the changes you approve, described next.

Search Console and Google Analytics are connected the same way, in Settings, each with its own Connect button and its own Google authorisation, which asks only to view that product’s data: https://www.googleapis.com/auth/webmasters.readonly for Search Console, and https://www.googleapis.com/auth/analytics.readonly for Google Analytics, both of which Google classes as sensitive. Both are read-only. Roster uses them only to read the figures for the reports you build, and never changes anything in Search Console or Google Analytics. Their tokens are stored encrypted in the same way.

Roster requests the https://www.googleapis.com/auth/adwords scope, which Google classes as sensitive, plus your Google account email address. The adwords scope permits changes. Roster makes one kind: when you ask it to set up a campaign or a conversion action, it shows you exactly what it will create, has Google check it without creating anything, and creates it only after you approve it in that form. Campaigns are created paused, so nothing runs or spends until you switch it on in Google Ads. Roster never edits, pauses, removes or switches on anything in your account.

To tell you whether your Google login can make changes in an account at all, Roster sends Google a request that Google checks and then discards, changing nothing.

Roster's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements. Specifically, we do not use Google user data for advertising, we do not sell it, we do not transfer it except as described in section 6, and we do not allow humans to read it except where you have asked us to for support, where it is necessary for security or to comply with law, or where the data is aggregated and de-identified. We do not use Google user data, or anything derived from it, to develop, improve or train generalised or non-personalised AI or machine-learning models, and we do not allow Anthropic (section 6) to do so.

You can revoke Roster's access at any time from your Google account permissions page, or by disconnecting that Google account in Settings inside Roster. Disconnecting deletes its stored token and the list of accounts it could reach. The accounts you chose for your clients stay with them, with their reports, and are read again once a login that reaches them is connected. Disconnecting does not withdraw the permission at Google, since you may use the same Google account elsewhere; do that on the permissions page.

4. Meta user data

You connect Meta Ads once, for your agency, in Settings, and the connection serves every client whose ad accounts it reaches. You sign in with Facebook Login for Business and Meta issues Roster a user access token for that Facebook account that lasts 60 days. We store that token encrypted at rest using AES-256-GCM and use it for two purposes: reading the Meta ads data needed to render your reports, and making the changes you approve, described below. When it expires, Roster asks you to connect again.

Roster requests these permissions: ads_read, to read the performance of the ad accounts you choose; business_management, to find the ad accounts held in your business portfolios so that you can choose among them (it permits changes to a portfolio; Roster only reads it); ads_management, to set up the campaigns you approve, and to see which Instagram accounts an ad account’s ads can run as; and pages_show_list and pages_read_engagement, to see which Facebook Pages an ad account can advertise as, so that its ads run as the right one.

Roster makes one kind of change: when you ask it to set up a campaign, it shows you exactly what it will create — every ad set and every ad — has Meta check the campaign without creating it, and creates it only after you approve it in that form. The campaign, its ad sets and ads are created paused, so nothing runs or spends until you switch it on in Meta Ads Manager. Its images and videos are added to the ad account’s media library, where Meta keeps them. If Meta refuses part of a campaign while Roster is creating it, Roster removes the paused campaign it had just started, and nothing else. Roster never edits, pauses, removes or switches on anything else in your ad accounts, Pages, Instagram accounts or business portfolios.

To tell you whether your Meta login can make changes in an ad account at all, Roster sends Meta a request that Meta checks and then discards, changing nothing.

Roster's use of data received from Meta complies with the Meta Platform Terms. We use it only to produce your reports and to set up the campaigns you approve. We do not sell or license it, use it for advertising, use it to build profiles of people, or transfer it except as described in section 6, and we do not allow humans to read it except where you have asked us to for support, where it is necessary for security or to comply with law, or where the data is aggregated and de-identified.

You can remove Roster's access at any time by disconnecting that Facebook account in Settings inside Roster. Disconnecting deletes its stored token and the list of ad accounts it could reach, and withdraws Roster's permissions at Meta, so Roster also disappears from your Facebook settings. The ad accounts you chose for your clients stay with them, and are read again once a login that reaches them is connected. You can also remove Roster from Meta's side, under Business integrations in your Facebook settings; Roster can then read nothing more, and the token it holds is useless until you disconnect it in Roster or ask us to delete it (section 12).

7. Shared report links

A report's own page, /r/<report-id>, needs you to be signed in. What you send a client is a share link, /s/<token>: viewable without signing in, read-only, and pinned to the figures as they were when you made it. The token is long and random, and we do not list or index these pages — but anyone who has the link can see that report. Roster keeps each link encrypted, so you can copy it again from the report or the client’s page. Treat a share link as you would treat the report itself, and revoke it from the report page, or delete the report, if it should stop working.

Share links carry your branding, not ours. They are measured like every other Roster page: when someone opens one, we record that it was opened, which of the report’s pages they moved to, and how long it was read, under the rules in section 8, and the same banner offers them the same choice about the analytics cookie.

8. Analytics and cookies

We measure how Roster is used, on this site and inside the product, with Google Analytics. We are telling you plainly what changed here: this used to run in a cookieless configuration, and it no longer does.

The old setup set no cookie and kept no identifier at all. It was also close to useless. With nothing linking one page to the next, every page you opened looked like a different person arriving, so we could count that reports get shared and count that people sign up, and never learn whether anyone who signed up ever got as far as sharing one. The questions that decide what we build next — where setup goes wrong, which step people give up on, what fails and how often — all need one visit to be recognisable as one visit.

So Google Analytics now sets a cookie holding a random identifier. What that buys is continuity: your page views in one visit join up, and if you sign in they join up with your next visit too. Alongside it we send an internal account identifier — an opaque string from our database, never your email or your agency's name — so that we can tell that the same account used the dashboard and the Claude connector, rather than counting you twice.

What we do not do

  • No advertising cookies, ever. Advertising storage, advertising user data and ad personalisation are switched off permanently in our configuration, and nothing on the consent banner can turn them on. Roster does not advertise and builds no audiences.
  • No cross-site tracking, and no sharing of anything with data brokers.
  • We do not sell anything to anyone.

Your choice

If you are in the EEA, the UK or Switzerland, analytics storage starts switched off and stays off unless you choose “Allow”. Nothing is stored on your device for measurement before you answer. Everywhere else it starts on, and choosing “No thanks” or “Don’t allow” turns it off, so no analytics cookie is kept.

On this website, a banner asks. In Roster, Create account asks once; answering is optional, and your account is created either way. Left unanswered, a banner in Roster asks instead, though not while you are setting up Roster in Claude. You can change your answer later in Settings, in Roster. Someone opening a share link is asked by the banner too (section 7).

Your answer is remembered in your browser’s local storage and applies only to that browser. This website and Roster keep their answers separately, so each asks once. Clearing your browser data asks again.

Google also publishes a browser add-on that opts you out of Google Analytics on every site, ours included.

What is recorded

  • which page or screen was opened, and in what order;
  • where a visit came from: the site or search engine that linked to us (Google Analytics records the referring address itself), the app a link was opened in, such as Instagram or LinkedIn, or a tag on a link we shared;
  • on this website: which buttons were shown and which were clicked, which questions were opened, the steps of the demo on the homepage, how far a guide was read, and how quickly each page loaded and responded to you;
  • approximately how many seconds a page, or a section of a page, was visible and in front of you;
  • product events — a client added or kept, a Google Ads or Meta account connected, a Meta invite asked for, branding saved, a file attached or removed, a client profile saved, a report asked for, picked up by Claude and finished, a report shared, a report link opened by a client and which of its pages were opened, a request to Claude succeeding or failing, a campaign asked for, picked up by Claude, drafted, created or checked, a comment added — with a count or a short label (such as “demographics” for a page) where one is meaningful;
  • for campaigns, the platform (Google Ads or Meta), an outcome and short labels such as how long it took or whether a note was written — never a campaign’s name, ID or figures, or the text of a note or a comment;
  • a random identifier for your browser, and our internal account identifier if you are signed in;
  • rough shape-of-account facts in bands rather than exact figures: whether you have connected Google Ads at all, and whether you have one client or several;
  • coarse technical context supplied by any browser request — device type, browser, language, and an approximate location derived from the IP address, which Google discards rather than storing.

What is never sent

  • your name or email address;
  • your clients’ names, or any report name, note for Claude, comment or Facebook profile you have typed;
  • Google Ads or Meta account IDs, campaign names, or any advertising figure;
  • error messages. When something fails we send a category — “permission”, “quota”, “not found” — and never the message itself, because those messages quote what was being asked for. The full text stays in our own database.

The cookies Roster sets itself are the two described in section 2 — your sign-in session and the short-lived one used while connecting Google or Meta. Both are strictly necessary to log in at all, and neither is used for measurement.

On this website, once analytics is allowed, your browser also keeps one label saying where you first arrived from (such as “linkedin”) in its local storage, so later visits are counted against it. The browsers of Roster’s own team carry one more cookie, which keeps our own visits out of the figures.

Activity inside Roster

Separately from what your browser sends, Roster's servers record what is done inside the product:

  • Actions in the signed-in dashboard — adding a client, connecting Google Ads or Meta, asking for, saving, arranging or sharing a report, a share link being opened, asking for a campaign, checking its status or commenting on it — are sent from our servers to Google Analytics under our internal account identifier, with the same exclusions as the list above: no names, no emails, no figures.
  • Every request Claude makes through the Roster connector is recorded in our own database: which tool was called, what it was asked (its arguments, which can include the client, campaign or report it was about, the report design or campaign draft itself, and a comment Claude adds to a campaign), how long it took, whether it succeeded, the error if it failed, and a short summary of the result — counts, and the IDs and names of anything it created. It never includes the figures a request returned. Google Analytics receives only the tool’s name, whether it succeeded, roughly how long it took, and on failure a category such as “quota”.

We use these records to keep Roster working — to see what fails and why, and to fix it — and to decide what to build next. Neither sets anything on your device, so neither depends on the banner above: they are part of running the service you signed up for. The connector record is visible only to us and deleted after 90 days (section 9).

9. How long we keep it

  • Account and client records — until you ask us to delete them.
  • Google refresh tokens — until you disconnect that Google account in Settings or your Roster account is deleted. A token you revoke at Google stops working at once; Roster keeps it only to tell you which login to reconnect.
  • Meta access tokens — until you disconnect that Facebook account in Settings or your Roster account is deleted. Meta stops accepting a token after 60 days, or as soon as you remove Roster at Meta; connecting again replaces it.
  • Account lists — what each Google or Facebook login can reach, until you disconnect it; each new read of the list replaces the last.
  • Meta invite requests — until your Roster account is deleted, or sooner if you ask us (section 12). Asking again adds a request, and the earlier ones are kept too.
  • Ad accounts chosen for a client — until you remove them from it, move them to another client, or delete the client.
  • Reports you ask for, with the note for Claude — until you delete the report or its client. A request you cancel stays, marked cancelled, until you delete its client.
  • Files and client profiles — a file, with what Roster read from it, until you remove it, its report or client is deleted, or the request it is on is cancelled; a client’s profile until its client is deleted.
  • Report snapshots — retained as history so past reports remain viewable. Deleted with the report or the client.
  • Sessions — expire automatically, and are destroyed when you sign out.
  • Connector requests (the record of Claude’s requests in section 8) — 90 days, after which each record is deleted automatically.
  • Record of changes — the campaigns and conversion actions Roster set up for you, and each campaign’s page: the request, the latest draft, what you approved, the last status reading, Things to look at and comments, until you delete the client or your account; a comment until you delete it. It stays if you only disconnect Google or Meta, so you keep a record of what was done in your accounts.
  • Claude connections — until you sign out of the connection in Claude, or 30 days after it was last used.
  • Branding — until you change or remove it. A logo you replace or remove is deleted when you save; a client’s logo is deleted with that client.
  • Analytics — 14 months, the retention period configured in Google Analytics, after which the event-level records are deleted and only aggregate totals remain. Analytics records carry a random browser identifier and, when you are signed in, our internal account identifier; ask us and we will delete yours.

Deleting a client deletes the ad accounts chosen for it, its branding and logo, its profile and files, its reports (requests, their notes and files included) and their snapshots, and its record of changes, including its campaigns’ pages and their comments. Your Google and Meta connections stay in Settings, because they serve your other clients. Campaigns already created in Google Ads or Meta stay there; delete them in Google Ads or Meta Ads Manager.

10. Security

  • Google refresh tokens, Meta access tokens and share-link tokens are encrypted at rest with AES-256-GCM.
  • Passwords are stored as salted scrypt hashes and are not recoverable.
  • Traffic is served over HTTPS.
  • Session cookies are httpOnly and sameSite, and sessions are revoked server-side on sign-out rather than left to expire.
  • Each client's data is scoped to its owning account in the database query itself, so a mistaken identifier returns nothing rather than another agency's records.

No system is perfectly secure. If you believe you have found a vulnerability, please write to gauravkr.contact@gmail.com before disclosing it publicly.

12. Deleting your data

You can delete most of what Roster holds yourself, without asking us:

A request Claude hasn’t built can be cancelled there instead; it goes when its client does. Disconnecting alone leaves reports you have already made, and their figures, in place.

To delete your whole account, or anything Roster holds about you or a Facebook or Google login you used with it, email gauravkr.contact@gmail.com from the address you sign in with, or tell us the Facebook or Google account concerned.
